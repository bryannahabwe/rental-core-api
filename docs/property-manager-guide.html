Property Manager Guide

Everything you can do as a Property Manager.

You look after the day-to-day on the properties you're assigned — tenants, units, agreements and payments. This guide walks through each screen you'll use and what you can do on it.

00 Your workspace

What you can reach — and what stays with the office

When you sign in you're taken straight to Tenants, already focused on a property you manage. Your menu is deliberately short: it holds the tools for running a property, and hides account-wide administration.

In your menu

✓ Tenants — the people in your units
✓ Units — the rentable spaces
✓ Agreements — tenancy & billing terms
✓ Payments — record and track rent

Handled by owners / admins

– Dashboard & Reports
– Properties
– Users & Activity log
– Settings

Managing more than one property? Use the property switcher at the top of the menu to move between the properties assigned to you. Everything on every screen — tenants, units, payments — reflects the property you've selected.

01 Tenants

The people in your units — and exactly what they owe

Your home screen. Every tenant on the selected property is listed here, with their current balance already worked out for you.

What you can do

Add a tenant

+ Add Tenant records a person — name, phone, email and (optionally) address.

What to expect: they show up in the list immediately, but with no unit, rent or balance — a tenant is just a contact until you link them to a unit with an Agreement (section 04). Until then their row reads “No agreement”.
Search & filter

Type in the search box (name, phone or email), or tap PAID / PARTIAL / UNPAID to narrow by payment state.

What to expect: the list filters as you type; a Clear button appears to reset it. Long lists page with “Page N of M”.
Read the balance

Every tenant shows a green “Fully paid up” or a red “Outstanding” bar with the amount owed and how far they've paid.

What to expect: when someone owes, the amount is split into historical arrears (what they brought in from before) and current cycles — so you can tell “behind from before” apart from “behind this month”.
Open a tenant / View

Tap any row, or View, to open their full page.

What to expect: contact details plus their complete ledger — see the next section.
Ledger

Ledger jumps straight to a tenant's transactions & arrears.

What to expect: a read-only history — opening it changes nothing.
Edit a tenant

Edit corrects the tenant's name, phone or email.

What to expect: contact details only. Their unit, rent and balance are driven by the agreement, not edited here.

Removing a tenant record is reserved for the account owner, so there's no delete button on your view — ask your owner or admin if something needs deleting.

02 Tenant details

One tenant, the full picture

Opening a tenant shows their contact details, their current tenancy, and a complete money trail — every billing cycle and every payment.

What's on the page

This page answers one question — “has this tenant paid, and for which months?” — without you doing any maths.

Contact

Phone, email and address on file for the tenant.

Current tenancy

The live agreement in summary: unit, monthly rent, the current billing period, and what's outstanding.

What to expect: “Outstanding” shows red with an amount when they're behind, or a green “Paid up” when they're clear — mirrored by the status pill.
Ledger & arrears

The full money trail: an arrears banner, totals (expected / paid / outstanding), every billing cycle, and every transaction (date, amount, period, method, source, reference).

What to expect: cycles that aren't due yet are greyed “not yet due”; overdue cycles are flagged red; a payment that overpaid shows an “X applied · Y rolled over” note. It's a record — you add money from the Payments screen, not here.
Edit

The header Edit updates the same contact details as the list.

What to expect: name and email are managed here as contact info; rent and tenancy come from the agreement.

03 Units

The rentable spaces on your property

Every room or unit, its monthly rent, and whether it's available or occupied.

What you can do

Add a unit

+ Add Unit creates a rentable space — room number, monthly rent, an optional description, and an “Available for rent” switch.

What to expect: only units marked Available can be chosen when you create an agreement, and the rent you set here pre-fills that agreement's rent (you can still override it).
Search & filter

Search by room number, or filter Available / Occupied.

Edit a unit

Edit updates the rent, description or availability.

What to expect: you rarely need to flip availability yourself — creating an agreement marks a unit Occupied automatically, and recording a move-out sets it back to Available.

Deleting a unit is owner-only, so there's no delete button on your view.

04 Agreements

Who rents what, on which terms

An agreement links a tenant to a unit and sets the rent, the billing model and the move-in date. It's what every ledger is built on.

What you can do

Create a new agreement

+ New Agreement links a tenant to an available unit and starts the billing clock. You choose new vs existing tenant, the unit and rent (pre-filled from the unit), the billing model, the move-in date, and an optional deposit.

What to expect: the unit is marked Occupied, and rent is billed on the same day each month as the move-in date — move in on the 5th → due on the 5th (any day after the 28th bills on the 28th).
  • Advance vs Arrears — Advance means rent is due at the start of each cycle; Arrears means it's due at the end.
  • Existing tenant → Opening balance — if they're joining mid-history, set how much they've paid ahead (+) or owe in arrears (−) so their ledger starts from the truth instead of zero.
Edit an agreement

Edit adjusts the rent, billing day, deposit or opening balance.

What to expect: changes shape how upcoming cycles are billed — use it to correct a rent or fix a starting balance.
Record a move-out

Move-out ends a tenancy — you pick the move-out date (it can't be before the move-in date).

What to expect: the agreement becomes Terminated, the unit is freed back to Available, and no further payments can be recorded against it.
Filter & search

Switch between Active / Terminated / All, or search by tenant or unit.

05 Payments

Record rent and hand out receipts

Log every payment against the right billing cycle, and generate a receipt on the spot.

What you can do

Record a payment

+ Record Payment: choose the tenant/agreement, pick the billing cycle (the earliest unpaid month is pre-selected for you), enter the amount, and save.

What to expect depends on the amount:
  • Pay the full rent → the cycle turns PAID.
  • Pay less → the cycle is PARTIAL and the shortfall stays as their outstanding balance.
  • Pay more → the extra rolls over automatically onto the next cycle(s) as a ROLLOVER credit — you'll see “X applied · Y rolled over”.
  • A receipt is generated that you can download right away.
A terminated agreement can't take payments — record everything before you move a tenant out.
Write a manual receipt

The Manual Receipt tab produces a standalone receipt for a one-off — e.g. a cash handover you just need a document for — in a Digital (clean, branded) or Formal (receipt-book) style.

What to expect: you type the details (tenant, amount, optional period and balance) and get a downloadable receipt; it's for documenting, separate from the cycle-tracking flow above.
Find a payment

Search by tenant, unit or reference, or narrow with the From / To date range.

Download a receipt

Receipt on any row re-opens that payment and lets you download its receipt again.

Everything on this screen is available to you — no owner sign-off needed.
